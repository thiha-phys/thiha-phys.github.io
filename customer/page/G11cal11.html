Rev.2
Q:

Rev.2.   Explain how an n-type semiconductor and a p-type semiconductor can be obtained. What are the majority carriers in the above semiconductors?

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:   When arsenic atoms having five valence electrons are added to the pure semiconductor (Ge,Si) atoms having four valence electrons, the conductivity of pure semiconductor increases. Since the number of electrons is greater than that of positive holes in this impure semiconductor it is called an n-type semiconductor ('n' stands for negative). In the n-type semiconductor, electrons are the majority charge carriers of electric current.

  When indium atoms having three valence electrons are added to pure semiconductor (Ge,Si) atoms having four valence electrons, the conductivity of pure semiconductor increases. Since the number of positive holes is greater than that of electrons in this impure semiconductor it is called a p-type semiconductor ('p' stands for positive). In the p-type semiconductor, positive holes are the majority charge carriers of electric current.

Rev.3
Q:

Rev.3.   What are meant by forward biased and reverse biased? Explain these terms using circuit diagrams.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:   Forward biased

  When a battery is joined with its positive terminal to the p-type semiconductor and its negative terminal to the n-type semiconductor, positive holes from p-type semiconductor pass through the junction easily. Thus, a current flows through the p-n junction diode. The p-n junction is now said to be forward-biased

  Reverse-biased

  When a battery is joined with its negative terminal to the p-type and its positive terminal to the n-type, only a very small current (reverse current due to minority carrier) flows through the p-n junction diode. In this case the p-n junction is said to be reverse-biased.

No.2
Q:

No.2.   Distinguish between half-wave and full-wave rectification.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:   Half-wave rectifier

  Only one diode is used in the half-wave rectifier.

  During the first half of the cycle, a is at a higher potential than b. The current flows in the circuit.

  During the second half of the cycle, a is at a lower potential than b. No current flows in the circuit.

  The current I flows in the diode for every first half of the cycle of AC (or) for every half-wave.

  Full-wave rectifier

  Two diodes are used in the full-wave rectifier.

  During the first half of the cycle, a is at a higher potential than b. The current I1 flows in the diode D1 and no current flows in the diode D2.

  During the second half of the cycle, b is at a higher potential than a. The current I2 flows in D2 and no current flows in D1.

  Since D1 and D2 operate alternately for one cycle of an AC voltage, current always flows through the resistor R.

  As the current flows through R for both half-cycle of the AC voltage (or) for a full-wave.

Q:

Rev.6.   Explain how a transistor can be used as a current amplifier and as a power amplifier.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:   Current amplifier

  IC = collector current

  IB = base current

  IE = emitter current

  IE = IC + IB

  The majority of positive holes flow across the base to the collector. The remainder of them flows across the base.

  The base is so thin that IB \(~\) 0.02 IE and IC \(~\) 0.98 IE.

  Therefore, the small base current IB can control a very large collector current IC. Because of this property a transistor can be regarded as a current amplifier.

  Power amplifier

  The resistance of forward-biased emitter junction is small and that of reverse-biased collector junction is large. But IC is nearly equal to IE. Since the electrical power is I2 R, the power in the emitter side is small whereas the power in the collector side is large. Therefore, the transistor can be regarded as a power amplifier.
