Rev.3
Q:

Rev.3.   Why does a nucleus possess the positive charge?

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:   The nucleus consists of two kinds of particles called proton and neutron. A proton is a positively charged particle and a neutron is an uncharged particle. Thus, the nucleus has net positive charge.

Rev.4
Q:

Rev.4.   What do you understand by a bound electron and a free electron?

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:     Bond electron

    The inner electron that is tightly bound by the nucleus (or) an electron closer to the nucleus is called a bound electron.

    Free electron

    The outer electron that is loosely bound by the nucleus (or) an electron far away from the nucleus is called a free electron.

Rev.6
Q:

Rev.6.   There are very large numbers of charged particles in most objects. Why then, don't most objects exhibit static electricity?

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:   In a normal atom, the number of the orbiting electrons is always equal to the number of protons in the nucleus. Since the magnitude of the positive charge of the nucleus is equal to that of the total negative charge of the electrons. So, a normal atom has no net charge and most objects do not normally exhibit static electricity.

Rev.7
Q:

Rev.7.   If the balloon near a wall is charged by rubbing with hair, the charged balloon will stick to a wall. Explain this phenomenon.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:     When the balloon is rubbed with human hair, a certain amount of electrons passes from the hair towards the balloon, and the balloon becomes negatively charged body. The negative charges in the balloon repel the free electrons in the wall, leaving positive charges near to the balloon. The attractive force becomes larger than the repulsive force, and the balloon will stick to the wall.
