Rev.3
Q:

Rev.3.   Discuss the essential differences among Thomson's model, Rutherford's model and Bohr's model of an atom.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:   In Thomson’s model, an atom might be a spherical volume of positive charge with electrons embedded inside it like currant in a bun (or) plums in a pudding.

  In Rutherford’s model, the atom must be largely empty space with all of its positive charges and most of its mass concentrated in a small region. This concentrated volume at the centre of the atom is called the nucleus. Negatively charged electrons are moving around the tiny nucleus.

  In Bohr’s model, electrons move in circular orbits around the nucleus. Only certain orbits are allowed in this model. The electron is never found between the allowed orbits. However, it can jump from one orbit to another. Bohr assumed that the atom does not emit energy in the form of radiation when the electron is in an allowed orbit. Hence the total energy of the atom remains constant.

Planck constant, h

= 6.626 × \({10}^{-34}\) J s

= 6.626 × \({10}^{-34}\) J s \(×\frac{1 eV}{1.6 × {10}^{-19} J}\)

= 4.142 \(× {10}^{-15}\) eV s

\(λ\) = 0.5 μm = 0.5 × \({10}^{-6}\) m= 5 × \({10}^{-7}\) m

1 eV = 1.6 × \({10}^{-19}\) J,     h = 6.626 \(× {10}^{-34}\) J s,   c = 3 × \({10}^{8}\) m s-1

E

= h f

= h \(\frac{c}{λ}\)

E

= 4 × \({10}^{-19}\) J × \(\frac{1 eV}{1.6 × {10}^{-19} J}\)

= \(\frac{6.626 × {10}^{-34} ×3 × {10}^{8}}{5 × {10}^{-7}}\)

= 2.5 eV

Q:

Eg.2.     The energy of a certain incident ray is 4.14 eV. What is the frequency of this incident ray?

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

1 eV = 1.6 × \({10}^{-19}\) J,     h = 6.626 \(× {10}^{-34}\) J s

E = 4.14 eV = 4.14 eV × \(\frac{1.6 × {10}^{-19} J}{1 eV}\) = 4.14 ×1.6 × \({10}^{-19}\) J

E

= h f

f

= \(\frac{E}{h}\)

= \(\frac{4.14 ×1.6 × {10}^{-19}}{6.626 × {10}^{-34}}\) = 1 × \({10}^{15}\) Hz
