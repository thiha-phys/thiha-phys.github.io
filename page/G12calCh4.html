Eg.1

Q:  At present day, the rate of formation of fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas) is estimated to be about 4 billion kW (4 × l09 kW) and the world's consumption of energy is of the order of 1017 Btu/year. Show that the rate of consumption of fossil fuel is of the same order of magnitude as the rate of their formation and these energy sources are therefore non-renewable.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

1 Btu = 1055 J

1 year = 365 \(\times \) 24 \(\times \) 3600 = 3.15 \(\times \) 107 s

 

The rate of formation of fossil fuel

 

 = 4 billion kW = 4 × l09 × l03 W

 

 = 4 × l012 J s-1

 

The world's consumption of energy

 

 = 1017 Btu/year = \(\frac{{10}^{17}\,\mathrm{Btu}}{1\,\mathrm{year}}\)

 

 = \(\frac{{10}^{17}\,\times \,1055\,J}{1\,\times 3.15\,\times \,{10}^{7}\,s}\)

 

 = 3.35 \(\times \) 1012 J s-1

 

Hence, the rate of consumption of fossil fuel is of the same order of magnitude as the rate of their formation and these energy sources are therefore non-renewable.

Eg.2

Q:  A home requires 62 kWh of heat on winter day to maintain a constant indoor temperature of 20\(℃\). Assume that the temperature of hot water outlet is 60\(℃\). (i) How much collector surface area does it need for a solar heating system that has a 20\(\%\) efficiency? (ii) How large does the storage tank have to be to provide this much energy? (The average solar radiation per day in winter is about 6.5 kWh m-2)

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

\({∆Q}_{\mathrm{req}}\)= 62 kWh, \({∆Q}_{\mathrm{input}}\) = 6.5 kWh m-2

\(\eta \) = 20\(\%\) = 0.2

(i)

\(\eta \,\)

=

\(\frac{{∆Q}_{\mathrm{output}}}{{∆Q}_{\mathrm{input}}}\)

 

\({∆Q}_{\mathrm{output}}\)

=

\({∆Q}_{\mathrm{input}}\) \(\times \) \(\eta \)

 

 

=

6.5 kWh m-2 \(\times \) 0.2

 

 

=

1.3 k Wh m-2

 

Collector surface area

=

\(\frac{{∆Q}_{\mathrm{req}}}{{∆Q}_{\mathrm{output}}}\)

 

 

=

\(\frac{62\,\mathrm{kWh}}{1.3\,k\,\mathrm{Wh}\,{m}^{-2}}\)

 

 

=

47.69 \(\approx \) 48 m2

(ii)

1 k Wh

=

3.6 \(\times \) 106 J

 

\({∆Q}_{\mathrm{req}}\)

=

62 kWh = 62\(\times \) 3.6 \(\times \) 106 J

 

\(∆\)T

=

60 – 20 = 40\(℃\) = 40 K

 

c = 4184 J kg-1 K-1

 

\({∆Q}_{\mathrm{req}}\)

=

m c \(∆\)T

 

m

=

\(\frac{{∆Q}_{\mathrm{req}}}{c\,∆T}\)

 

 

=

\(\frac{62\,\times \,3.6\,\times \,1{0}^{6}}{4184\,\times 40}\) = 1333.6 kg

 

Volume of storage tank should be 1334 L.

Rev.4

Q:  The band gap of GaAs is 1.4 eV. Calculate the minimum wavelength of light for photovoltaic generation in a GaAs solar cell.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

E = 1.4 eV = 1.4 \(\times \) 1.6 \(\times \) 10-19 J, \(\lambda \) = ?

h = 6.626 \(\times \) 10-34 J s, c = 3 \(\times \) 108 m s-1

 

E

=

\(\frac{h\,c}{\lambda }\)

 

\(\lambda \)

=

\(\frac{h\,c}{E}\)

 

 

=

\(\frac{6.626\,\times \,1{0}^{-34}\,\times \,3\,\times \,{10}^{8}}{1.4\,\times \,1.6\,\times \,{10}^{-19}\,\,}\)

 

 

=

8.87 \(\times \) 10-7

 

 

=

887 \(\times \) 10-9 m (or) 887 nm

Rev.5

Q:  A 100 ft \(\times \) 50 ft building has a flat roof. What is the average solar energy received by the roof in a month? (The solar radiation per day in winter is about 1600 Btu ft-2)

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

A = 100 ft \(\times \) 50 ft = 5000 ft2

t = 1 month = 30 days

 

Solar radiation per day = 1600 Btu ft-2

 

Energy received by roof in month

 

= 1600 Btu ft-2 \(\times \) 5000 ft2 \(\times \) 30

= 240 000 000 = 24 \(\times \) 107 Btu

Rev.6

Q:  Calculate the solar energy received by a standard hot water collector of dimensions 1 m \(\times \) 2 m, over one hour at around noon, it the irradiance stays fairly constant at about 800 W m-2.

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

A = 1 m \(\times \) 2 m = 2 m2, t = 1 h = 3600 s

 

Solar radiation = 800 W m-2 = 800 J s-1 m-2

 

Solar energy received by 2 m2 in 1 h

 

= 800 J s-1 m-2 \(\times \) 3600 s \(\times \) 2 m2

 

= 5.76 \(\times \) 106 J

Eg.4

Q:  At a hydroelectric power plant, the water pressure head is at a height of 300 m and the water flow available is 100 m3 s-1. If the turbine generator efficiency is 60\(\%\), estimate the electric power available from the plant. (g = 9.8 m s-2, \(\rho \) = 1000 kg m-3)

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

h = 300 m, \(\overset{̇}{V}\) = 100 m3 s-1, \(\eta \) = 60\(\%\) = 0.6

g = 9.8 m s-2, \(\rho \) = 1000 kg m-3, P = ?

 

P

=

\(\eta \) \(\rho \) \(\overset{̇}{V}\) g h

 

 

=

0.6 \(\times \) 1000 \(\times \) 100 \(\times \) 9.8 \(\times \) 300

 

 

=

176.4 \(\times \) 106 W = 176.4 MW

Q:  A dam which maximum head of 200 m produces to 2000 MW electrical power. What is the rate of falling water on the turbines? The turbine generator efficiency is 50\(\%\). (Density of water = 1000 kg m-3).

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

h = 200 m, \(\overset{̇}{V}\) = ?, \(\eta \) = 50\(\%\) = 0.5

g = 9.8 m s-2, \(\rho \) = 1000 kg m-3

P = 2000 MW = 2000\(\times \) 106 W

 

P

=

\(\eta \) \(\rho \) \(\overset{̇}{V}\) g h

 

\(\overset{̇}{V}\)

=

\(\frac{P}{\eta \,\rho \,g\,h}\)

 

 

=

\(\frac{2000\,\times \,1{0}^{6}\,}{0.5\,\times 1000\,\times \,9.8\,\times \,200}\) = 2040 m3 s-1

Q:  The hydro turbine receives water from a reservoir at an elevation of 100 m above it. What is the minimum water flow in kg s-1 to produce a steady turbine output of 50 MW if the turbine generator efficiency is 75 \(\%\)?

Ans: အဖြေနှင့် တွက်နည်းကြည့်ရန် နှိပ်ပါ

Ans:

h = 100 m, \(\eta \) = 75\(\%\) = 0.75

P = 50 MW = 50 \(\times \) 106 W

 

P

=

\(\eta \) \(\rho \) \(\overset{̇}{V}\) g h

 

P

=

\(\eta \) \(\rho \) \(\frac{V}{t}\) g h

 

P

=

\(\eta \) \(\frac{m}{t}\) g h  (\(\because \) m = \(\rho \) V)

 

\(\frac{m}{t}\)

=

\(\frac{P}{\eta \,g\,h}\)

 

\(\frac{m}{t}\)

=

\(\frac{50\,\times \,1{0}^{6}\,}{0.75\,\times \,9.8\,\times \,100}\) = 68027 kg s-1
